## 10b. Selling this to a hundred customers — what that changes

**Owner, 2026-09-06:** *"if i have to deploy this from git to 100 people i need
to have 100 diff compose files with their emails?"*

That question reframed the product. Everything below was a "someday" while this
was one contractor's deployment and is not one now. **None of it is a feature.
Each is a thing that must be settled before customers exist, because each gets
harder with every customer added.**

### 10b.1 The deployment must be identical for every customer

A per-customer compose file is not a product. `LOCAL_USER_EMAIL` currently has
to name a `users` row before anyone can sign in, so a fresh install cannot
bootstrap itself.

**The fix, agreed in shape:** in local mode the variable becomes optional.
Set, it is used as now. Unset with **no** users, no identity is established
and the setup wizard runs anyway, since it deliberately sits outside the
permission guard. Unset with **exactly one** active user, that user is the
owner, resolved from the database and cached in process the way SSO sessions
already are in `src/lib/auth/session.ts`. Unset with **more than one**, refuse
loudly — ambiguous is the branch that must never be guessed, because guessing
picks somebody's account.

Every customer then gets a byte-identical image and compose, and the first
person through the wizard becomes the owner. It is an authentication change:
all four branches want tests, the refusal most of all. Roughly 1.5 hours.

### 10b.2 The backup key stops being a conversation

§10 above describes a customer holding backups he cannot decrypt. One customer
is a conversation. A hundred is a liability, and partly a legal question rather
than a technical one. `2026-09-04-licensing-design.md` §5.5 proposes encrypting
to two recipients and handing the customer his private half on paper with the
hardware.

**This blocks a commercial launch rather than merely being unresolved.**

### 10b.2a The app should generate the backup key itself

**Owner, 2026-09-06:** *"shouldnt backup key be generated by the app itself?
maybe in enable backup page."*

Yes. Today `deploy/synology/README.md` tells the installer to run `age-keygen`
on a different machine and paste the public half into compose. That is a
correct instruction and almost nobody will follow it: it needs a tool they do
not have, on a computer they are not looking at, in the middle of setting up a
NAS. The observed result is `BACKUP_AGE_PUBLIC_KEY` left empty, the backup
container in `Exited(1)`, and a deployment taking no backups -- which is what
happened on the first real install.

**The shape, and the one thing it must not do.** An "Enable backups" screen
generates the pair server-side, writes ONLY the public half to
`/data/config`, and shows the private half exactly once, on screen, with a
copy button and a print button. It is never stored, never logged, never put
in a database row (rows are mirrored to SharePoint and land in every backup,
which is §10's whole problem), and never shown again. The screen does not
advance until the person confirms they have saved it.

That the private key briefly exists in the app's memory is the cost. It is
worth paying: the alternative on the evidence is no key at all, and a backup
nobody can decrypt is strictly better than a backup nobody took. What must
never happen is the private half being *persisted* anywhere on the NAS,
because that is the threat the encryption exists for -- somebody carrying the
box out of the office.

**Where it sits.** Part of the pending-items panel in 10b.5 rather than a
separate feature: "backups: not configured" is one of the things that panel
is for. Related to 10b.2, which is about the owner holding a key he cannot
lose; this is about him having one at all.

**Also from that install:** the backup container's refusal is designed to be
loud -- *"Exited(1) beside a running app is visible in one `docker compose
ps`"*. In Synology Container Manager it is not. The owner saw a container that
"never comes up", clicked Terminal, and got *"Failed to attach. No teletype
terminal found."* -- a message about ttys, for a container that had exited
with a perfectly clear reason in a log tab he had no cause to open. A design
that relies on where a failure is visible has to name the UI it is visible in.

### 10b.3 Licensing

### 10b.4 Updates

A hundred NAS boxes each needing a 1.3GB tar copied by hand is a support

### 10b.5 A fresh install must lead itself to the wizard

**Owner, 2026-09-06,** on the first-run screen of his own NAS deploy: *"also
add it to bring up a setup wizard to go through setup pending things to do"*.

With no `organization` row, `src/app/page.tsx` renders a "Setup required"
notice and stops. Two things are wrong with it, and both only show up on a
customer's machine rather than a developer's.

**It names a command the reader cannot run.** *"Load the demo tenant with `npm
run db:seed`"* is written for somebody at a checkout. A contractor looking at
a NAS in a browser has no shell, no repository and no npm, so the one
instruction on the screen is the one thing he cannot do. The other half --
*"or complete the setup wizard"* -- does not say where the wizard is and is
not a link, even though `/setup` is three characters away.

**It stops rather than leads.** The wizard is a nine-step ORDER precisely
because "which of these forty fields must I fill in first" is a question a
menu cannot answer (`src/app/setup/steps.ts` says so in its own header). A
first-run screen that presents no next step hands that question straight back.

**The fix, in three parts:**

1. **Send them there.** With no organization and setup unfinished, `/`
   redirects to `setupHref(gate.resumeAt)`. A redirect rather than a link,
   because there is nothing else on that screen to choose instead; the wizard
   already sits outside the permission guard, so nothing new is exposed.
   `readSetupGate` already refuses a company it did not create, so a live
   tenant whose organization read merely failed cannot be walked into these
   forms.
2. **Show what is left.** The data exists today: `readSetupGate` returns
   `completed` and `resumeAt`, so a panel listing the nine steps with the
   finished ones ticked needs no new query and no new state. It answers "how
   much more of this is there" -- the question that makes a person abandon a
   wizard halfway.
3. **Say it in the reader's terms.** The seed command belongs behind a
   development-only condition or nowhere. What a customer needs on that screen
   is one sentence and one button.

Sequenced **after 10b.1**, not beside it. Until `LOCAL_USER_EMAIL` is
optional, a fresh container still refuses to start without an address, so a
customer never reaches this screen to be led anywhere. Roughly 1 hour once
10b.1 lands.

Related, and cheap enough to fold in: when setup is finished but pieces are
still missing -- no tax rate covering the current year, no rate items, no
users beyond the owner -- the same panel is where that belongs. Deliberately
NOT a second wizard. One list, on the screen the owner already opens first.

## 11. Deferred features, with the reason

### Phase 3, in this order

**1. Crew.** The owner, 2026-09-05: *"no i don't have people on payroll but
might in future, so crew is genuine."*

A third kind of person: an employee who is scheduled onto work by name and
never signs in. A `users` row for them is a dormant login that exists for
nothing; a `vendors` row files an employee as a counterparty under a tax regime
that has nothing to do with payroll. `assignments.ts` argues for `users`, and
that argument is right *until* there is a payroll — which is why this is Phase
3 and not never.

The seam is already built: `src/lib/schedule/roster.ts` was written to absorb a
third kind with one added query, and nothing downstream of it branches on the
kind of assignee. The work is a table, a settings screen, and widening the
assignment CHECK from "vendor or user" to `num_nonnulls(vendor_id, user_id,
crew_id) = 1`.

The 72-line `src/db/schema/crew.ts` written by an agent is **deleted rather than
kept**: it was never wired, never migrated, and its columns would be rewritten
against whatever the schema looks like by then. The reasoning above is the part
worth keeping, and it is here.

**2. The estimator role.** Owner, 2026-09-05: *"i don't want to do estimator in
this phase, then maybe add estimator in phase 3 but to be done before ap ar."*

**Before AP/AR, and the order is the point.** AP/AR adds a payments table and
screens showing what is owed, paid and outstanding — every one of them a cost
surface. With `cost:read` already in place, each is gated as it is written, by
whoever is already in the file. Built the other way round, somebody has to
return to finished screens and retrofit a permission, and a retrofitted
permission is the kind that gets missed silently: a page with no gate looks
exactly like a page that works.

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-estimator-role-design.md`. Its central
claim is that hiding a column does not hide the number — cost reaches the
browser in the RSC payload, so the loaders must return a shape without it.

**3. AP/AR.** Deferred by the owner on 2026-09-05. Creating an invoice from a
job already works; what is missing is recording payment. A `payments` table with
status derived from the sum, never a paid flag — for this business partial
payment is the normal case (a deposit, progress draws, a holdback released
sixty days later), so a boolean would be wrong within a week and would answer
neither "how much is outstanding" nor "since when".

### Also deferred

- **Outbound email** (Phase 2 §2.4) — deferred by the owner in favour of a
  log-an-email action. Trigger for revisiting: evidence he uses the reminder
  list. Sending on his behalf before then is worse than nothing.
- **SharePoint mirror, Plan 4b** — **cut from Phase 1** by the owner on
  2026-09-04. The seam exists (`NotImplementedMirror`), the settings screen
  exists, the sync does not. Nothing depends on it. *Still to record in the
  Phase 1 roadmap doc.*
- **OCR on receipts** — the columns (`source`, `ocr_*`) exist and are unused so
  that adding it later is not a migration against a live table. Engine is
  proven and portable per the reuse spec.
- **PDF receipts render inline** — currently download-only, which is the safe
  answer. Inline would need a sandboxed frame and a tightened CSP.
- **Drag to move a task** — argued against and deferred. It conflicts with
  showing what will move BEFORE it moves, and touch-dragging a bar next to
  other bars at 390px is unreliable. If revisited, the honest version is drag
  on the calendar only, for a task with no dependents.

## 11b. Designed 2026-09-07, not scheduled

Two designs written at the owner's request, both explicitly for later. Listed
here because a spec nobody links to is a spec nobody reads.

### 11b.1 Two companies under one owner

`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-07-two-companies-design.md`. Reviewed by Fable.

The owner asked whether he could quote from two sister companies -- a repair
business and a home builder -- with per-company requirements. The design's
first finding is that **what he described wanting is a simpler form, not a
second company**, and it splits accordingly: Wave A is simple mode keyed on
project type (1-3 days, delivers the stated need), Wave B is the `companies`
table and the `organization` split (a few days), Wave C is the per-company
visibility wall (deferred to the estimator role, since staff are months away).

**Three things in it have a deadline** rather than merely being unscheduled,
because they are cheap in the next migration and expensive as a retrofit:
`projects.company_id`, `document_sequences` rekeyed by company, and
`tax_rates.company_id`. Anyone touching those tables before Wave B should read
§4.3 first.

**A correction to §8 item 2 of that spec, made 2026-09-09.** It claims
unscoped tax rates are a live hazard regardless of Wave B. **That is wrong
about the mechanism.** `computeTaxes` date-filters through
`selectRatesInForce`, and applying every in-force rate is DELIBERATE -- a
GST+PST province needs exactly that, which is what `isCompound` is for. So
unscoped rates only misbehave when two companies each hold a full rate set in
force at once, which is a Wave B prerequisite and not a standing bug.

The real standing defect is narrower, and it is §12's new entry: `addTaxRate`
has no overlap guard.

### 11b.3 Service work and contract work, and starter data per trade

`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-09-service-and-contract-work-design.md`.

**NEXT UP, ahead of the launch-blockers, by the owner's decision 2026-09-09.**

Supersedes §3 of the two-companies design, which argued simple mode should key
on the job "not the company". The owner corrected it: the two businesses have
different WORKFLOWS, not different form lengths. So there are two levels --
company posture decides which MODULES exist, project type decides which FIELDS
appear inside one.

The vocabulary is **service work / contract work**, not "builder". His
objection is the reason: *"can this app not be used for other services like
electrician plumber?"* An electrician, a plumber and a home builder all have
the same split inside their own business, so the axis is the work rather than
the trade -- and it is real industry language, which is the only test that
matters for a word on a first-run screen.

Separately, the trade IS asked, and selects a **starter pack** of project
types, cost codes, rate items and line groups. It gates nothing at runtime.
Two rules in that section are not scope decisions:

- **Packs contain no prices.** A rate book pre-filled with invented numbers is
  worse than an empty one, because it looks authoritative. Structure is the
  gift; arithmetic is the owner's.
- **Do not ship MasterFormat.** CSI holds copyright on the compilation and
  this is a product being sold. Plain-language divisions instead.

Also records that hiding holdback by company can produce a legally wrong
document -- the Construction Act attaches it to the improvement, not the
contract size -- so posture sets a default a job can override, never a hard
removal.

### 11b.2 Who owns the code fields

`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-07-generated-codes-design.md`.

Rate item codes get allocated by the app when the field is left blank --
`PLM-03` from the cost code, inside the insert transaction, the way quote
numbers already work. Cost codes stay typed, because a chart of accounts has
to agree with the accountant's, and only gets a suggested child code from its
parent. Half a day for both.

The importer's `code` field and its alias list (`itemcode`, `sku`, `ref`...)
are the reason a supplied code is always honoured: the owner's rate book
already exists and already has codes.

## 12. Smaller things, recorded so they are not rediscovered

- **A quote prints holdback terms on a job that withholds nothing.** Found by
  Fable 2026-09-09 while reviewing an unrelated spec, and verified.
  `src/app/print/quote/[id]/page.tsx:357-361` prints
  `org.holdbackTermsText` unconditionally and never reads
  `quotes.holdbackPctTenThou`. **The settings screen argues for the opposite
  behaviour in as many words** -- `src/app/settings/financial/page.tsx:132`:
  *"Only a default, not a company-wide rate: it is stored per quote, so a job
  that withholds nothing prints no holdback block."* That sentence describes
  behaviour the code does not have. Set the org default to 0% while terms text
  stands and every quote tells the customer 10% is being retained when nothing
  is. A wrong statement about money on a customer-facing document, and the
  reasoning that justified the design is the thing that is false. The print
  block must read the quote's own percentage.
- **Holdback can be accrued and never released.** Found the same way, verified.
  The engine supports the `holdback_release` invoice kind fully
  (`src/lib/invoice/compute.ts:52,167`, `repository.ts:282`) and
  `src/app/projects/[id]/billing/page.tsx:66` has a label for it -- but the
  only action that creates an invoice takes
  `z.enum(['progress', 'final'])` (`billing/actions.ts:34`). So there is no
  path in the product that releases a holdback. It accrues on every draw and
  sits in the ledger as money owed and un-invoiceable. `deposit` and
  `change_order` are unreachable for the same reason.
- **`addTaxRate` has no overlap guard.** Found 2026-09-09.
  `src/app/settings/tax-rates/actions.ts:59` inserts a rate with no check
  against what is already in force. The SUPERSEDE path is careful -- it closes
  the old row at `effectiveFrom - 1` and refuses a row that was already closed
  -- but nothing stops somebody reaching for "Add" instead and creating a
  second open-ended `HST`. Both are then in force, `computeTaxes` correctly
  applies both, and every quote from that day carries 26% tax. Silent, on a
  customer-facing document, and the arithmetic is not at fault. The guard
  belongs on the add action: refuse a label whose window overlaps one already
  in force, and name Supersede as what was meant.
- Reminder rule titles repeat the card they sit on ("Won Basement finish —
  confirm start date" under a card titled *Basement finish*). Right on the
  reminders screen where the project is not in view; wrong on a pipeline card.
  Fix at the template: lead with the verb.
- Days-in-stage could carry a tone threshold. **This no longer needs a number
  from the owner**, and asking for one was the wrong question: the thresholds
  already exist as reminder rules (`quote_sent` + 3 days, `no_activity` + 14),
  maintained at `/settings/reminder-rules`. A second setting meaning the same
  thing would disagree with the first the day one of them changed. The owner's
  own suggestion is the right shape — a pipeline card looks concerned when the
  job has an OPEN reminder against it, which is the same fact the reminders
  screen shows and cannot drift from it.
- The pipeline's kind filter could be one-tap links instead of a select.
- `Quote sent` column could carry the summed value of sent, unexpired quotes,
  labelled "quoted" to distinguish it from contract value.
- Column headings on the board are not sticky at desktop widths (they are below
  `sm`). Matters once a column is long.
- `purchase_order_id` on `assignments` has no foreign key, because
  `purchase_orders` does not exist yet.
- `expenses.project_id` is `NOT NULL`, where the spec left it nullable —
  overhead with no job is currently unsupported rather than half-supported.
- `expense_taxes.is_recoverable` is a boolean, so partial recovery (a
  50%-claimable meal) cannot be expressed.
